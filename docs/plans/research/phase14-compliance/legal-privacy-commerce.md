## FLAGS THAT CONTRADICT THE PLAN AS DESCRIBED
1. **Pre-checked marketing box (US).** Lawful under CAN-SPAM (no consent needed) but: (a) invalid for Canada (CASL), and for any other admitted country requiring opt-in; (b) a dark-pattern/"consent" risk in states with consent-defined-terms (CCPA/CPRA and ~20 state laws define valid consent as a clear affirmative act and exclude pre-ticked boxes / dark patterns, relevant if the email list feeds targeted advertising or sale); (c) if any visitor is a 13-17 teen, several state laws require opt-in for targeted ads/sale. Recommendation to counsel: do not pre-check for any non-US visitor and consider un-checked for all.
2. **"Admit everyone but EEA/UK/OFAC".** OFAC list needs a correction: Syria is no longer comprehensively sanctioned (program terminated 1 Jul 2025). Switzerland is not blocked and has its own extraterritorial data law (FADP). Canada (CASL), Brazil, Australia, Japan, Korea, India all admitted. Russia/Belarus/Venezuela are not country-wide embargoes but carry heavy restrictions. Geo-block reduces but does not eliminate GDPR scope (Topic 6).
3. **13+ sign-up.** COPPA only binds under 13, so 13+ is workable, but: the site sends a face photo of a person to an AI engine (biometric-sensitive), teens 13-17 trigger state teen-privacy duties, and a 13-17 user cannot upload a photo (18+ for upload) which is consistent. Age-screen design must meet FTC guidance (Topic 3). A "friend" photo upload (photo of a third party, possibly a minor) is the larger gap: the uploader is 18+ but the depicted person may be a child; COPPA does not reach this (collected from the adult, not the child), but BIPA/CUBI/state sensitive-data laws do not exempt it. Counsel question.
4. **Biometric exposure is the largest legal risk in the plan** (BIPA statutory damages; "AI photo editor" suits are active). Blocking Illinois, or a documented BIPA-style consent plus retention schedule, is the main design decision for counsel (Topic 1).
5. **Session replay / pixels:** California reform (SB 690) removed only the private right of action for pen-register claims, not wiretap/recording claims (Topic 5).
6. **Click-to-Cancel federal rule is dead (vacated); state ARLs are now the operative strict regime**; CA AB 2863 applies to contracts after 1 Jul 2025 (Topic 8).

## 1. Biometric privacy for face photos sent to an AI image generator

### 1.1 Illinois BIPA (740 ILCS 14)
(a) Plain English: A private entity may not collect/capture a "biometric identifier" or "biometric information" without (1) written notice of the collection and purpose and retention period, and (2) a written release; must have a public written retention/destruction policy (destroy at earliest of purpose satisfied or 3 years after last interaction); no sale/profit; no disclosure without consent. Private right of action: $1,000 negligent / $5,000 intentional/reckless per violation plus fees.
(b) Cites: 740 ILCS 14/10 (definitions), 14/15 (duties), 14/20 (damages). URL: https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=3004 (not fetched). Definition `[recalled]`: "'Biometric identifier' means a retina or iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or scan of hand or face geometry." Photographs are expressly excluded: `[recalled]` "Biometric identifiers do not include ... photographs" (the exclusion covers the photo itself, not a face-geometry template derived from it).
(c) Dates/status: 2024 amendment (SB 2979, signed 2 Aug 2024) added 14/20(b)-(c): repeated collection of the same biometric identifier from the same person by the same method is a single violation; electronic signature counts as written release. Seventh Circuit, Clay v. Union Pacific R.R. (decided 1 Apr 2026 per Sidley/McGuireWoods alerts) held the amendment applies retroactively to pending cases; Illinois state courts largely held otherwise, and the Illinois Supreme Court has the final word (pending/possible; unable to verify: Illinois Supreme Court docket/any rehearing). Source: https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/04/08/seventh-circuit-limits-potential-damages-under-bipa-holds-2024-amendment-applies-retroactively/
Photo-derived face data decisions found (secondary sources, verify dockets):
- Doe v. Apple (S.D. Ill., Photos app): face crops alone are not biometric identifiers; a derived mathematical representation could be a "scan of face geometry"; summary judgment largely denied to Apple (MLex).
- Seventh Circuit (reported Aug 2026, Samsung Gallery): BIPA does not reach face data generated and kept on the user's own device; court assumed without deciding the data is a biometric identifier (Mayer Brown alert, 20 Aug 2026). Implication: sending the photo to a server/third-party engine is the opposite fact pattern (the operator and vendor have control).
- Zellmer v. Meta (9th Cir. 2024): face geometry that cannot identify a person is not a BIPA "biometric identifier" (reported; verify citation).
- Voila AI Artist (cartoon-face app): BIPA class action dismissed with prejudice on personal jurisdiction, not merits (Lawstreet).
- Suits against generative-AI image editors alleging face-geometry capture (Grok/xAI-type complaints) reported Oct 2026 as filed, no merits ruling found.
- Dismissal for lack of identification (X Corp, N.D. Ill.): "biometric identifiers must identify individuals" (Inside Privacy).
(d) What it requires: if a photo is converted to any face embedding/landmark/template by the site or its engine (or the engine runs face detection/identity-preservation models), assume BIPA applies to Illinois residents. Duties: just-in-time written notice and consent (checkbox that is not pre-checked) naming purpose, retention period, and vendors; a public retention schedule (destroy no later than purpose satisfied/3 years; the site's 30-day purge is compatible and should be stated); no sale/disclosure beyond consent; deletion mirrored at the engine vendor. Privacy Policy/Terms/UI: a dedicated "Face and photo data" section; consent modal at first upload.
(e) Counsel: (i) does the pipeline create a face template at all (engine-side?); (ii) block Illinois at upload or consent and proceed; (iii) arbitration/class-waiver enforceability for BIPA; (iv) insurance; (v) "friend" photos: consent from depicted third party who is not a user; (vi) retroactivity outcome in Illinois Supreme Court.

### 1.2 Texas CUBI (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 503.001) and TRAIGA
(a) Must inform and obtain consent before capturing a biometric identifier for a commercial purpose; no sale; reasonable care; destroy within a reasonable time, not later than the first anniversary of purpose expiry. No private right of action: Texas AG only, up to $25,000 per violation.
(b) Statute https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/BC/htm/BC.503.htm (not fetched). Definition `[recalled]`: "'Biometric identifier' means a retina or iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or record of hand or face geometry."
(c) TRAIGA (HB 149) effective 1 Jan 2026 amended § 503.001 (per Perkins Coie/Alston/FBT Gibbons summaries): online availability of a person's image is not by itself consent unless the individual made it public; and an exception for processing biometric identifiers in developing, training, evaluating, or offering AI systems unless used to uniquely identify a specific individual. TRAIGA itself: AG-only enforcement, no private action. unable to verify: enrolled text of amended § 503.001 (statutes.capitol.texas.gov).
(d) Consent notice on upload; retention <= 1 year after purpose; the AI-offering exception likely helps if no identification, but do not rely without counsel.
(e) Counsel: scope of the AI exception for a face-likeness generator (likeness is arguably "identifying").

### 1.3 Washington
RCW 19.375 (biometric identifiers, enrollment in a database for commercial purpose requires notice/consent; AG enforcement through CPA, no private action by its terms; unable to verify: text from app.leg.wa.gov). My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373): covers "biometric data" as consumer health data when used to identify health; private right of action via CPA; effective 31 Mar 2024 (small business 30 Jun 2024). Reach to a humor face-image site is doubtful unless data relates to health status; counsel. Washington "biometric identifier" definition excludes physical/digital photographs `[recalled]`. unable to verify: current text, any 2025-26 amendments.

### 1.4 Other states with private action
Illinois is the only broad biometric statute with a private right of action found. Colorado's 2025 biometric amendment to CPA (HB 24-1130, effective 1 Jul 2025) and state comprehensive laws list biometric data as "sensitive" (consent required, AG-only). unable to verify: whether any other state added a private biometric right in 2025-26 (check NCSL/IAPP tracker).

### 1.5 Vendor and wording practice
Contract with the image-engine vendor: (1) zero-retention or deletion within defined days (match the site's 30-day purge, with deletion API call on user delete); (2) no training/fine-tuning on inputs/outputs; (3) no human review other than abuse/safety; (4) subprocessor list and sub-processor flow-down; (5) no biometric template creation for identification, or bear BIPA responsibility; (6) security (SOC 2), breach notice 72 hours; (7) data location; (8) indemnity; (9) audit/evidence of deletion; (10) no onward use of faces. Comparable products' consent language (typical): "I consent to you using my photo to create AI images, including processing by our service providers; photos deleted within X days; we never sell or use photos to train AI." unable to verify: current wording of named competitor sites (not searched, privacy-rule).

## 3. Children and teens

### 3.1 COPPA and amended Rule (15 U.S.C. §§ 6501-6506; 16 C.F.R. Part 312)
(a) COPPA applies to operators of (i) sites/services directed to children under 13, or (ii) general-audience sites with actual knowledge they collect personal information from a child under 13; also "mixed audience" sites may age-screen neutrally. Verifiable parental consent before collection; notice; parental access; retention only as long as necessary; security.
(b) 16 C.F.R. § 312.2 (definitions, "directed to children" factors: subject matter, visual content, use of animated characters, music, age of models, child-oriented activities, ads directed to children, competent evidence of audience). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-312 (not fetched).
(c) Amended Rule: published 22 Apr 2025, 90 Fed. Reg. 16918; effective 23 Jun 2025; compliance deadline 22 Apr 2026 for most provisions (confirmed by multiple alerts; primary page not read) https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule. Compliance date has PASSED. Changes widely reported (unable to verify each against primary text): separate verifiable parental consent for disclosure to third parties (incl. for targeted ads/AI training); written information-security program; written data-retention policy and prohibition on indefinite retention (§ 312.10); expansion of "personal information" to include biometric identifiers (faceprints etc.) and government IDs; new consent mechanisms; mixed-audience clarifications. Exceptions § 312.11(d)(1), (d)(4), (g) (safe-harbor-related) have a different timeline.
(d) What it requires: A meme/humor site with anime/cartoon-like imagery or "friend" content may attract child audiences; FTC weighs actual audience. Neutral age screen at sign-up is the accepted tool for general-audience/mixed-audience sites (FTC COPPA FAQ H.x, unable to verify numbering): ask date of birth/age with no hint of the cut-off; do not pre-fill; no encouragement to lie; block re-entry after a failure with a session cookie/persistent flag (no back-button retry); do not collect other PI from a child who self-identifies as under 13; delete any PI collected; if actual knowledge acquired later (support email, user says "I'm 12", a parent reports) delete the account and data promptly or obtain VPC. ToS: state 13+; Privacy Policy: Children's section, parent contact. Retention schedule: written policy required under amended rule if COPPA-covered.
(e) Counsel: whether the product is "directed to children" (young-skewing humor), a photo of a child by an adult user (not collected "from" the child but state laws and the biometric statutes still bite); FTC posture on AI face generators.

## 4. Marketing email and non-US regimes

### 4.1 CAN-SPAM (15 U.S.C. §§ 7701-7713; 16 C.F.R. Part 316)
(a) Commercial email: no prior consent required in the US (opt-out regime). Must have: accurate header/from info; non-deceptive subject; identify as an ad where required; valid physical postal address; clear opt-out; opt-out honoured within 10 business days; opt-out mechanism works 30 days after send; no fee/more than minimal info to opt out. Transactional/relationship messages (receipts, security, account notices) exempt from most rules but not from false headers. Penalty up to ~$53k per email (FTC 2025 figure; unable to verify current inflation adjustment).
(b) 15 U.S.C. § 7704(a)(3), (a)(5); 16 C.F.R. § 316.5 (10 business days). https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-316 .
(c) Pre-checked box is not unlawful under CAN-SPAM. It is a consent-quality problem elsewhere (Topic 4.2, CCPA-type "consent" definitions) and the FTC may view as unfair if combined with misleading text. (d) Footer with postal address and unsubscribe; one-click list-unsubscribe header (Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender rules, 5,000+/day, not statutory); keep suppression list.

## 6. Geo-blocking as GDPR/UK GDPR avoidance
(a) GDPR Art. 3(2): applies to a non-EU controller if it offers goods/services to data subjects in the EU, or monitors their behaviour in the EU. EDPB Guidelines 3/2018 (final v2.1, 12 Nov 2019): "envisaging" to offer to persons in the EU is judged by evidence (EU languages, currencies, delivery, EU domain, marketing to EU); mere accessibility is not enough; geo-blocking and clear statements may count as evidence of no targeting but are not conclusive. https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-32018-territorial-scope-gdpr-article-3-version_en (not fetched).
Quote `[recalled]` (Art. 3(2)): "...the processing activities are related to: (a) the offering of goods or services... to such data subjects in the Union; or (b) the monitoring of their behaviour as far as their behaviour takes place within the Union."
(c) UK: ICO treats UK GDPR territorial scope in parallel; ICO has said no special rule for geo-blocking. unable to verify: ICO statement (ico.org.uk guidance on territorial scope).
(d) Residual: EU resident travelling in the US is not "in the Union" (GDPR applies to data subjects while in the Union; a visitor physically in the US is out of Art. 3(2) on that reading, but EDPB scope focuses on location at time of offer). VPN users: the site did not target them; low risk. EU persons depicted in friends' photos: the friend (a US user) uploads; no offer to EU persons; but if the site processes biometric data of EU residents shown in photos, Art. 3(2)(b)'s monitoring limb is a stretch; low. Enforcement against small US firms with no EU establishment/representative is practically rare. Art. 27 representative duty applies only if Art. 3(2) applies.
(e) Switzerland: FADP art. 3 (effects principle) applies to foreign conduct with effects in Switzerland; Swiss FDPIC does not require geo-block; admitting Swiss is lower-risk than the EEA but not zero. Recommendation for counsel: either block CH too (cheap) or treat as lower risk with a statement in Terms. Counsel questions: "not offered in EEA/UK" language and a checkbox; whether the EU-friend-depiction scenario requires a Terms prohibition on uploading photos of persons located in the EEA/UK without consent.

## 8. Auto-renewing membership and credits
### 8.1 Federal
ROSCA (15 U.S.C. §§ 8401-8405): § 8403 `[recalled]` requires, for online negative-option sales, (1) clear and conspicuous disclosure of all material terms before obtaining billing information, (2) express informed consent, and (3) simple mechanisms to stop recurring charges. No NPRM found. unable to verify: events after Mar 2026 (check federalregister.gov for "Negative Option Rule"). Enforcement continues under ROSCA.
### 8.2 States
California ARL, Bus. & Prof. Code § 17600 et seq., as amended by AB 2863 (applies to contracts entered, amended, or extended on or after 1 Jul 2025): present offer terms clearly and conspicuously before purchase (incl. free-trial conversion); express affirmative consent to the renewal itself (separate from other terms; keep verification of consent); acknowledgement with terms, cancellation policy and how to cancel (retainable); online cancellation "click to cancel" by the same medium; annual reminder for plans of one year or longer (and for trial conversions); notice of price change; prompt phone answer if phone cancellation offered; save offers regulated. Sources: Barnes & Thornburg, Fenwick, KTS Law alerts. https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov (statute not fetched).

## 9. Retention principles for the retention schedule
- FTC Act § 5 (15 U.S.C. § 45): FTC treats retaining data longer than needed, and failing to honour deletion promises, as unfair/deceptive; recent orders require retention schedules and deletion (e.g., Rite Aid, Blackbaud, Avast-type consent orders; unable to verify each). Make privacy-policy deletion promises truthful: the "30-day undo then purge" must be real, including backups and vendors.
- State laws: data minimisation/retention limits in Maryland (strict necessity), Minnesota (retention schedule required), California CPRA (§ 1798.100(a)(3): no longer than reasonably necessary, disclose retention period per category), Colorado/Connecticut/etc. (minimisation), Texas CUBI (1 year), BIPA (3 years/purpose), COPPA amended (written retention policy).
- Records you must KEEP: federal/state tax and financial records (IRS: 3 years typical, 7 for some; state sales tax 3-4 years); payment/Stripe records; chargeback evidence windows (Visa/Mastercard dispute 120 days, up to 540 days in some cases; Stripe's evidence window typically 7-21 days after dispute) so keep order/consent/login evidence at least 18 months; DMCA 17 U.S.C. § 512(c) takedown/counter-notices (keep records and repeat-infringer policy; no fixed statutory period); legal holds on notice of litigation; ROSCA/ARL consent records (CA: keep for the longer of 3 years or one year after termination, per § 17602 as amended; unable to verify).
- Typical periods (industry practice, not law): IP/rate-limit records hours to 30 days (security log up to 90); error logs 30-90 days; analytics 14 months (GA4 default 2 months/14 months); account data until deletion + 30 days; backups 35 days; email suppression list kept indefinitely (needed to honour opt-outs).
